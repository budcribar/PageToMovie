Act as a casting director and continuity supervisor for a film adaptation.

Your task: produce a CLOSED CAST list as JSON only — for production pinning
(portraits, book plates, multi-ref video). Do NOT rewrite the screenplay.

SOURCE OF TRUTH: the FOUNTAIN screenplay. It must already describe the book's story
world (cast, looks, visual medium). Optional BOOK text is supporting evidence only —
never override Fountain medium, cast membership, or identity with book-only guesses.

================================================================
GOALS
================================================================
1. List every character who appears ON SCREEN or SPEAKS (including silent leads).
   You decide the closed cast by reading the FOUNTAIN and BOOK only — there is no
   external name list, no forced candidate list, and no post-pass that invents names.
   Do NOT invent cast from scene headings (INT./EXT. place/time), transitions, camera
   directions, or bare action verbs. Those are staging, not people or animals.
2. Include pure voice-only narrators when they have dialogue cues.
3. Infer filmable looks from action description + book text (species, size, colors,
   wardrobe, age). Do not invent major plot or people who never appear.
4. Use stable keys: Character_<PascalOrSnake> from the character's story name.
5. Silent heroes often only appear in action lines (name + who they are) — still seed
   them even with no dialogue cue when they are clearly a story role.
5b. **BOOK / TITLE = CAST STATUS (HARD):** If the BOOK names a being (person or animal)
   as a story role in the title, cover, refrain, or body, they MUST get a Character_*
   seed. Dialogue is not required. Incomplete cast = missing that titled lead.
5c. **AGE-VARIANT CUES → LINKED SEEDS (HARD):** A cue prefixed YOUNG/TEEN/OLD before a name
   that also appears bare elsewhere (e.g. YOUNG NICK and NICK) is the SAME person at a
   different life stage, not a second character. Give the prefixed cue its own
   Character_* seed (its own description/visual_lock/voice — a child does not share an
   adult's face or voice), set that seed's `variant_of` to the base character's key, and
   set `age_band` on it (e.g. `child_8_9`, `teen_15_17`). The base adult/present-day seed
   keeps `age_band` unset or `adult`. Never fold an age-variant cue's lines into the base
   seed, and never invent an age-variant seed for a name that never has a prefixed cue.
5d. **NAME-SPELLING UNIFICATION (HARD):** If the same person's name is spelled two ways
   across the Fountain (a typo carried over from the source, e.g. dialogue text saying
   "Olsen" in one place and "Olson" in another for the same family) — this is one
   character, one seed, keyed on whichever spelling is more frequent. Do not create two
   seeds for a spelling variant of one identity.
6. **BOOK-FIRST LOOKS:** When BOOK text is provided, mine it for age, build, hair,
   eyes, face, clothing, era, and distinguishing traits. description + visual_lock
   must be concrete enough to drive portrait generation and prevent role swaps
   (e.g. confessor Narrator ≠ elderly Old Man). Never leave "as described in the
   screenplay" or empty looks for on-screen roles when the book has cues.
7. **ON-CAMERA POV:** First-person confessors / presenters who face the camera are
   display_name_policy ok_anytime (NOT never_on_screen). never_on_screen only for
   pure off-screen VO with no body on camera.
8. **AUDIENCE / PERFORMANCE (from book, general — not a fixed eye recipe):** Infer how
   this adaptation relates to the viewer and how speaking characters typically engage
   (e.g. first-person confessional direct address, objective third-person observation,
   picture-book storybook staging, asides vs looking at scene partners). Put a short
   film-level lock in performance_lock. Per character, optional performance_notes when
   that role has a special address pattern. Do NOT hardcode "eyes open facing camera"
   for every project — only state conventions the book/screenplay actually support.
9. **RENDER MEDIUM FROM SCREENPLAY (HARD):** Set `render_style_lock` from the FOUNTAIN's
   stated medium (title-page Notes, early Action, overall dramatic register). The screenplay
   must describe the book's visual world — illustrated picture-book vs photoreal period
   drama vs gothic live-action, etc. Use BOOK text only to support what the Fountain already
   encodes; do NOT invent cartoon vs photoreal from "there was a text file" or "there were
   image plates." If Fountain never states medium, infer from story content in Fountain+Book
   (e.g. Poe short story → photoreal gothic live-action; animal picture book → illustrated).
   One medium for all cast.
10. **SHARED UNIFORMS (HARD):** If two or more on-screen characters wear the same
   professional uniform/costume (police officers, soldiers, servants, sports team, etc.),
   do NOT re-describe the coat/hat/badge/insignia separately in each character's own
   description/wardrobe_always — that invites each portrait to independently reinvent the
   uniform and drift out of sync with the others. Instead emit ONE entry in
   wardrobe_lock_tokens keyed Wardrobe_<Group> with a single concrete, unambiguous
   description (name the exact silhouette — e.g. "rounded peaked cap", not "a hat"; name
   the badge shape/wording, not just "a badge"). Point every member character's
   wardrobe_lock at that key. Each member's own description/visual_lock should then cover
   ONLY what makes that person distinguishable — build, face, hair, age — never the shared
   garment. Characters who don't share a uniform with anyone need no wardrobe_lock at all.
   **CONCRETE, NOT COMPARATIVE (HARD):** wardrobe_lock characters render from text alone —
   no reference photo of one member is ever shown when generating another, so language like
   "distinct from Reynolds and Briggs" or "a unique face" gives the image model nothing to
   go on and every member converges on the same generic face. Every member needs its OWN
   concrete, self-sufficient anchors — specific age band, hair color/style, facial hair
   present or absent (and what kind), and face shape/texture — enough that a model reading
   ONLY that one character's text (never the others') would draw a different person each
   time. Prefer one hard, easy-to-verify anchor per member (e.g. "the only one with a
   mustache", "visibly younger and smoother-faced than the other two") over vague adjectives.

PRIORITY IF CONFLICTS:
1. Valid JSON matching the schema below
2. Fidelity to screenplay + book (on-screen cast complete; looks from book when present)
3. Closed cast (no unnamed "MAN #2")
4. One render medium for the whole film
5. Distinct faces for each on-screen Character_* (no shared generic pale man)

================================================================
OUTPUT (HARD) — JSON ONLY
================================================================
Return ONLY a JSON object (no markdown fences, no commentary):

{

FIELD RULES:
- render_style_lock (REQUIRED): one STYLE LOCK string for the whole film, derived from the
  FOUNTAIN medium (Notes / early Action / story register). Not from upload format.
- description: 1–3 dense sentences of filmable identity (age band, build, face, hair,
  eyes, skin, wardrobe, era/medium). Enough for a portrait model with no other context.
- visual_lock: 1–3 sentences of **constants that must not drift** (same face, hair,
  signature garment/feature). Explicitly distinguish from other cast when similar.
- wardrobe_always: sticky garments/colors only (not full plot costume changes). Do not
  duplicate a shared uniform here — use wardrobe_lock_tokens + wardrobe_lock instead
  (see SHARED UNIFORMS rule above).
- wardrobe_lock_tokens / wardrobe_lock: only emit when 2+ on-screen characters genuinely
  share one professional uniform/costume. Omit entirely for casts with no shared uniform.
- description / visual_lock / wardrobe_always: **filmable only** — species, size, age,
  coat/hair colors, markings, face shape, real garments/props with color/shape.
- **FORBIDDEN in description/visual_lock:** "as described in the screenplay",
  "as in the book", "see screenplay", empty or name-only stubs for on-screen roles.
- **NICKNAMES vs VISUAL (HARD):** Book titles, epithets, and personality labels belong in
  dialogue or the movie title — **never** in description, visual_lock, or wardrobe.
  Bad: “noodle head look”, “noodle-head silhouette”, “biscuit-head hat”, “not very bright”
  as a facial design instruction (vague personality is not anatomy).
  Good: “small black-and-white dog, floppy ears, bright eyes, soft rounded head shape,
  short coat; snug black-and-white pajama top and bottoms when dressed.”
- Do **not** quote title words as if they were physical features.
- Metaphorical wardrobe: only real garments shown in art/story (e.g. actual pajamas), not
  coat-as-metaphor sleepwear inventing extra clothes the art never draws.
- cast_kind: "individual" (default) or "group" / "chorus" / "ensemble" for a plural
  unnamed body (children, crowd, classmates). Groups are one seed, not invented names.
- display_name_policy never_on_screen: pure VO narrator — omit visual_lock or leave short.
- performance_lock (film-level): 1–3 sentences from BOOK + Fountain about audience relationship
  and default performance/staging (e.g. "first-person confessional often addresses implied
  listener/camera when speaking; other characters are observed objects"; or "objective
  picture-book staging; characters look at each other / scene action, not the viewer").
  Prefer conventions over anatomy recipes. Empty only if nothing can be inferred.
- performance_notes (per character, optional): only when that role differs from the film default.
- reference_image_placeholder: bare filename only under assets/characters/ (no folder path).
- source_image_pages: optional 1–3 page numbers from book --- PAGE N --- if book has plates
  and the character is clearly visible; else [].
- age_band / variant_of: see AGE-VARIANT CUES rule above. Omit both on a character who only
  appears at one life stage — most characters never need them.
- Include humans AND animals that appear on screen.
- Do NOT invent characters not supported by screenplay or book.
- Prefer names used in the Fountain (BUSTER, MOMMA, DADDY, NARRATOR).
- location_seed_tokens (REQUIRED when Fountain has INT./EXT. headings): one Loc_* per
  distinct place. description = filmable set design (not just the place name). visual_lock =
  constants that keep the room/street identity stable (architecture, materials, key props,
  default light quality) — never bake a single scene's time-of-day into the lock.
- **FORBIDDEN in location description/visual_lock:** character names as primary content,
  plot beats, "as described in the screenplay", empty or name-only stubs when the Fountain
  or BOOK has set-dressing cues.

11. **LOCATIONS / SETS (HARD):** Also emit `location_seed_tokens` for every distinct
   place that appears in Fountain scene headings (INT./EXT. place names). Day vs night
   of the same place is ONE location (do not freeze time-of-day into visual_lock).
   Keys: `Loc_<PascalOrSnake>` from the place name (e.g. Loc_Kirk_Street_Apartment).
   Each seed needs filmable set design — architecture, era, materials, light sources,
   signature props, color palette — concrete enough to keep rooms consistent across clips.
   Mine BOOK text for place description when present. Do NOT invent places that never
   appear as headings. Do NOT put characters in location seeds.

12. **GROUP / CHORUS CAST (HARD):** When several unnamed people act or speak as one body
   (e.g. "the eager children", "the crowd", "classmates", villagers, soldiers) and the
   story does **not** give them individual proper names to pin, emit **one** Character_*
   seed with `"cast_kind": "group"` (aliases: chorus, ensemble, crowd). Do **not** invent
   proper names (no ELI/CLARA-style pupils) for that plural body. Individual named roles
   use `"cast_kind": "individual"` (or omit — individual is the default).
   Group seeds still need description + visual_lock for the collective look (wardrobe/age
   range), but production will **not** require a single-face portrait pin for them.

================================================================
OUTPUT
================================================================
JSON only. No trailing commentary.
